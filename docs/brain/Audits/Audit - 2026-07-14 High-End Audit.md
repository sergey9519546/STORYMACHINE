# Audit — 2026-07-14 High-End Audit

**Directory:** `docs/audits/2026-07-14-high-end-audit/` (19 files: baseline
verification, five phases of scope/bibliography/competitive-industry/UX/
security-privacy/repository-reconstruction findings, gap and truth
registries, devil's-advocate checkpoints, a synthesis, and a coverage
ledger CSV).

**What it is:** a whole-repository audit (2026-07-14 through 2026-07-15,
scope: executable behavior, git history, product claims, architecture,
security/privacy, content integrity, data/evaluation, UX/accessibility,
operations, market evidence, legal-risk signals, strategic futures), run
under an evidence-bounded posture where "current code and executed
witnesses outrank narrative prose; noncompensable gates outrank weighted
scores." It carries a formal claim-intent manifest and evidence labels
(`[Fact—executed]`, `[Fact—code]`, `[Fact—repo]`, `[Fact—external]`,
`[Inference]`, `[Hypothesis]`, `[Gap]`, `[Risk]`).

**What it found and its verdict counts:** among its headline claims —
STORYMACHINE has strong bounded software mechanics but no established
real-writing validity for its headline score; the recovery, build-context,
and AI-resource boundaries contained confirmed critical defects; no direct
writer demand had been documented at the audit snapshot; none of three
proposed product futures was launch-eligible. Its `PHASE_2_REPOSITORY_RECONSTRUCTION.md`
finding **R2-C01** is load-bearing for the rest of the project: the
earlier "8,917 rules, ~5,701 from a bulk Wave 1191" story was shown
inaccurate — the rulebook was always 3,216 distinct pass-scoped constants
(now 3,217 — see [[Glossary]] "rulebook").

**What was NOT reproduced / not concluded:** it explicitly did not select a
product future, and marked its own 83.0-style scores as requiring an
adjacent owner-prior and correlated-criteria caveat — it is a
decision-analysis document, not a launch authorization.
