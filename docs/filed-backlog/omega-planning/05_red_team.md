# StoryMachine — Red Team Report (Workstream 05)

**Date:** 2026-08-07. **Mandate:** find what kills this project. Settled falsifications (Rust/WASM, belief tensors, prefetch, etc.) are not relitigated. Everything below attacks the *surviving* plan (v5 kernel + v6 consumer wedge).
**Method:** master plan and verdict read in full; competitive and regulatory claims verified by web search Aug 7, 2026; cost/latency claims attacked with arithmetic; every citation below is a real retrieved source. Where I estimate, I say so.

**Headline finding:** the plan's engineering is unusually well-falsified; its *business* load-bearing claims are mostly unverified, and two of them are wrong as stated (§6). The single deepest risk is not on the required list's surface: **the alpha's 30–60 minute story length may be too short for the engine's advantage to exist** — frontier long-context models can hold a 45-minute story in context, so the null-hypothesis arm can tie at exactly the length the alpha showcases (KR-2). Second-deepest: **the sellable dataset likely contains model-provider outputs whose terms of service encumber resale for training** — a day-one schema decision, nearly unretrofittable (KR-12, §3.3). Fix both now; they cost days now and the company later.

## 1. Kill-Risk Register

Scored P (probability this materially bites, 0–1) × S (severity 1–5, 5 = company-ending). Ranked by product. Kill criteria are named so they can be cited in standups without ambiguity.

### KR-1. The core loop isn't fun (P .45 × S 5 = 2.25)
**Narrative:** The market's revealed demand in this category is companionship, romance, and power fantasy — not dramaturgy. StoryMachine sells *constraint*: irreversibility, secrets you don't control, endings that take things away. Hilary Mason's GDC 2026 retrospective reports "conversational freedom doesn't guarantee narrative freedom" — but also that "constrained creativity with clear goals" is what works ([Mason & Todd, Apr 2026](https://medium.com/@hmason/the-state-of-ai-native-games-lessons-from-the-frontier-3e696a9e3279)) — so the bet is live, not settled. The problem: the founder is a systems-thinker building the product he wishes existed, and the corpus contains zero evidence any stranger wants it.
**Early warning:** blind fun scores from non-friends in Phase 0/3; D1; % of first stories finished; unprompted "can I play again."
**Mitigation:** Phase 0 must use 5–10 strangers on a Wizard-of-Oz build, not the founder playing against an LLM (see §6, claim 11 — the current Phase 0 design is a biased instrument). Ship the alpha world to a hostile audience (drama-fandom Discords, not HN).
**KILL CRITERION — "THE FUN FLOOR":** if after two full iteration cycles of the alpha world, first-story completion < 25% or D7 < 10% among activated users, stop consumer; the engine survives as middleware/pro-tool, the wedge does not.

### KR-2. The null-hypothesis arm ties — plain chat is just as good (P .40 × S 5 = 2.00)
**Narrative:** The benchmark's headline chart is "engine vs. strong-model-plain-chat-with-story-bible." A 45-minute story is ~15–40k tokens of transcript — comfortably inside any 2026 frontier context window. At that length, a strong model with the full transcript and a bible in context commits few contradictions and leaks few secrets; long-context coherence has kept improving since the corpus was written. The engine's structural guarantees (payoff scheduling, epistemic enforcement, endings) matter most on *long, multi-session* arcs — which the alpha deliberately excludes for scope. So the plan may publish a benchmark, at alpha length, showing its own engine within noise of a prompt. That chart, once produced, cannot be unpublished internally: it tells acquirers the kernel is telemetry, not product.
**Early warning:** run the harness at 45-min length *first, privately*, before any public benchmark; measure blind human preference engine-vs-null, not just violation counts.
**Mitigation:** design the benchmark's headline condition where the effect actually lives: 3+ hour multi-session runs, 8+ entities, 2+ concurrent secrets, delayed payoffs — and add a product surface (episodic serial stories, persistent worlds) that exercises it. Measure ending quality and payoff completion, which prompts genuinely flub, not just contradiction rate.
**KILL CRITERION — "THE NULL GATE":** if at alpha-representative length the engine's contradiction+leak advantage is < 2× over the null arm AND blind preference is ≤ 55/45, do not build the consumer surface on "coherence" claims — reposition the engine as long-arc infrastructure or compiler tooling within 30 days.

### KR-3. Solo-founder execution bandwidth (P .50 × S 4 = 2.00)
**Narrative:** The plan requires, simultaneously: a consumer app with live users, an authored world of professional quality, T&S operations with statutory crisis-protocol duties (§3.1), a published benchmark, dataset engineering, and eventually running an auction. That is four jobs plus a fifth (fundraising). The 2025–26 failure mode for solo AI founders isn't the code — agents write code — it's the *operational* surface: moderation queues, incident response, compliance filings, creator support. One person cannot be on-call for a consumer product where the statutory duty is suicide-referral protocols.
**Early warning:** phase exit criteria slipping > 4 weeks; T&S response backlog > 48h; founder doing support during build hours.
**Mitigation:** waitlist-gated alpha capped at a size one person can moderate (hundreds, not thousands); automated crisis routing from day one; a moderation contractor budgeted before Phase 3 launch, not after the first incident; ruthless deferral of the benchmark until Phase 4 as planned.
**KILL CRITERION — "THE BANDWIDTH BREAKER":** if Phase 2 exit (headless 5-scene run, zero contradictions, measured cost) is not achieved within 12 weeks of build start, or if at any point T&S/user-ops consumes > 30% of founder hours for a month, halt growth and either hire/contract the ops surface or shrink the alpha until it fits.

### KR-4. Cost-per-story blowout (P .35 × S 4 = 1.40)
**Narrative (with the arithmetic the plan never did):** A 45-minute story at ~1 beat per 30–45s is 60–90 beats. Per beat on a mid-frontier model at early/mid-2026-style list prices (~$3/M in, ~$15/M out — estimate, verify at build): ~4–6k tokens in + ~400 out ≈ **$0.02/beat**, plus cheap-model parse/gate, ~10% regenerates, and the compile. Total: **~$1.50–2.50 per completed story** on strong-model dramatization; ~$0.50–0.80 on a cheap-tier dramatizer (quality risk — quality *is* the product). A $10/mo subscriber playing 15 stories costs $22–37 in COGS: "a small fraction of subscription price" (master plan §3) is currently false at list prices without caps. And Mason's category lesson is that "token-based monetization walls frustrate players" — the cost fix and the fun fix pull in opposite directions. That squeeze, not any single number, is the risk.
**Early warning:** the already-planned cost meter: median and P95 cost per completed story; % of tokens on strong model; stories abandoned at cap.
**Mitigation:** price at $15–20/mo with a generous-feeling energy system framed diegetically ("the troupe rests"); route harder (only C-adjacent beats get the strong model); use the preference dataset to fine-tune an open-weight dramatizer (Latitude's Wayfarer — a Llama 3.3 70B storytelling finetune — proves the path: [AI Dungeon/X, 2025](https://x.com/aidungeon/status/1892296610589397469)).
**KILL CRITERION — "THE MARGIN LINE":** if after one routing-optimization pass the median completed story costs > $1.00, or unit economics require > $25/mo to reach 60% gross margin at observed play frequency, freeze feature work until routing/fine-tune closes it; if it cannot close within a quarter, the consumer business model is dead and the engine goes B2B.

### KR-5. Safety incident (P .25 × S 5 = 1.25)
**Narrative:** This category's incidents are not hypothetical: wrongful-death suits against Character.AI reached settlement posture in 2026 with families still filing ([Open Class Actions, 2026](https://openclassactions.com/lawsuits/character-ai-google-teen-harm-lawsuits-settlement.php)). StoryMachine's *product* is betrayal, lies, death, and irreversible acts, dramatized — a screenshot of the engine staging a suicide beat for a distressed user, or any confirmed minor on the platform, is company-ending for an unfunded solo founder, and now carries statutory private rights of action in multiple states (§3.1). Mason: "safety/moderation crises destroy trust overnight."
**Early warning:** red-team eval pass rate in the harness (adversarial self-harm/minor-coded prompts); crisis-classifier trigger logs; age-assurance failure sampling.
**Mitigation:** 18+ only at alpha with real age assurance (not a checkbox); crisis detection and referral implemented *as if* companion laws apply even where arguably exempt (§3.1); dark-theme dramatization rails (the C-tier confirmation pattern is genuinely also a safety pattern — the plan is right about this); incident runbook written before launch; no romance/NSFW at alpha, accepting the demand cost.
**KILL CRITERION — "THE BRIGHT LINE":** any confirmed incident involving a minor, or any crisis-protocol failure with real-world harm → immediate public-access freeze, external review before relaunch. Structurally: if age assurance can't hold estimated minor leakage < 2% of actives, do not open public signup.

### KR-6. Incumbent fast-follow (P .60 × S 3 = 1.80)
**Narrative:** Everything demoable is copyable at 80% fidelity in one quarter: a "state panel" (prompted summary), "endings" (Character.AI's Stories format already has them — [TechCrunch, Nov 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/25/character-ai-will-offer-interactive-stories-to-kids-instead-of-open-ended-chat/)), "export your story as a script" (transcript summarization). Users cannot measure contradiction rates; they see features. C.AI has 28M MAU ([Mason & Todd 2026](https://medium.com/@hmason/the-state-of-ai-native-games-lessons-from-the-frontier-3e696a9e3279)); Sekai ships 200,000 user-created apps *per day* ([GlobeNewswire, Jun 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/06/03/3306441/0/en/sekai-raises-26m-in-seed-and-series-a-as-users-create-play-and-remix-15-million-mini-apps.html)).
**Early warning:** incumbent product announcements containing "endings," "memory," "your story, kept."
**Mitigation:** see §2 — the durable asymmetry is that *endings are anti-engagement* for session-time businesses, plus dataset/benchmark/velocity. Publish the coherence vocabulary first so their copies get graded on your ruler.
**KILL CRITERION — "THE PARITY TRIGGER" (reposition, not stop):** if an incumbent ships state-tracked endings + compiled artifact at perceived parity (blind side-by-side ≥ 45% preference for theirs), collapse the roadmap to the two uncopyable assets (dataset, benchmark) and open acquisition conversations immediately rather than out-featuring a 28M-MAU platform.

### KR-7. The compile artifact underwhelms — nobody shares it (P .45 × S 3 = 1.35)
**Narrative:** The entire growth loop hangs on people sharing a *screenplay*. Screenplay format is sacred to writers and alien to the consumer audience; a text share-page has near-zero native reach in feeds that reward vertical video. Sekai's growth came from making play *watchable*; a Fountain-formatted PDF-alike is proof-of-quality to an acquirer and a shrug to a 22-year-old. The plan calls the compile "the strategic weapon" — it may be a diligence exhibit, not a growth engine.
**Early warning:** artifact-share rate; share→visit→signup conversion; qualitative "would you post this?"
**Mitigation:** treat the *screenplay* as the acquirer-facing artifact and build a second, feed-native compile target from the same state: a 30–60s "trailer of your run" (title card, three beats, your fatal choice, ending name) — the compiler architecture supports multiple projections by design; use it.
**KILL CRITERION — "THE SHARE FLOOR":** if after testing two artifact formats, share rate < 5% of completions or shares drive < 0.05 new activations per completion, demote the compile from strategy weapon to finale-screen feature and find the actual growth loop before scaling spend.

### KR-8. The confirmation mechanic annoys players (P .30 × S 3 = 0.90)
**Narrative:** C-tier interrupts are designed rare — but tier *classification is done by a cheap model*, and mis-tiering is the failure: a B-beat staged as portentous drama reads as camp; a missed C-beat kills trust in the "it asks before irreversible things" promise. Worse, the A-tier ticker's one-tap revert is under-specified: reverting a silently-committed fact after five beats have referenced it requires retroactive state surgery the plan never addresses (§6, claim 13). Undo-that-contradicts is worse than no undo.
**Early warning:** quit rate within 2 beats of a C-interrupt; revert usage and post-revert contradiction reports; decision latency at C-beats.
**Mitigation:** revert windows expire after N beats or trigger a visible reconciliation; C-tier staging playtested as *drama* (diegetic phrasing, not modal admin); tier-classifier precision tracked in the harness with golden cases.
**KILL CRITERION — "THE WEIGHT TEST":** if > 15% of players who hit a C-tier interrupt quit within 2 beats, or median C-decision time > 20s with negative sentiment, replace interrupts with diegetic in-fiction choice framing in the next sprint; if that also fails, the confirmation mechanic becomes invisible (auto-commit + prominent revert) and the "human-confirmed truth" law survives only in the pro tool.

### KR-9. Dataset value overestimated — labs don't buy (P .55 × S 3 = 1.65)
**Narrative:** The plan calls the dataset "the asset labs want... Nobody has this." Uniqueness ≠ demand. The 2026 human-data supply side is crowded and vendor-dominated (Scale/Surge/Mercor-class vendors and ~50 funded human-data startups — [AlignList 2026](https://alignlist.com/guides/top-50-human-data-startups); [Troveo 2026](https://www.troveo.ai/resources/ai-training-data-marketplace)); labs *commission* bespoke preference data to exact spec at will. An alpha with hundreds–low-thousands of users yields perhaps 10⁵–10⁶ choice events — small against commissioned pipelines. There is no public comparable transaction for "state-conditioned narrative preference data." The honest case for the dataset: organic, longitudinal, real-stakes choices with ground-truth state — which commissioned data genuinely lacks — plus its *internal* use fine-tuning the cheap dramatizer (KR-4 mitigation). That's real value; it is not an auction pillar at alpha scale.
**Early warning:** actual expressions of interest (data partnership, eval-license) from any lab or vendor after Phase 4 outreach.
**Mitigation:** keep consent/provenance pristine so the option stays alive (§3.3); frame it in the deck as multiplier, not pillar.
**KILL CRITERION — "THE COMPARABLE TEST" (valuation, not stop):** if after three serious lab/vendor conversations there is no term-sheet-shaped interest, strike the dataset as a standalone exit asset from all materials and value it only as internal COGS-reduction.

### KR-10. Latency kills immersion (P .25 × S 3 = 0.75)
**Narrative:** The happy path is fine: intent parse (~0.3–0.7s cheap model) + state ops (ms) + strong-model TTFT (~1–2s) ≈ 1.5–3s to first streamed token — tolerable with staging. The unhappy path is the trap: the fast post-generation gate with one bounded regenerate *doubles* full-beat latency (20–40s of dead air) precisely on the most dramatically loaded beats, where violations are likeliest. The plan's own principle ("optimistic streaming") is violated by its own QC gate exactly when tension peaks.
**Early warning:** P95 TTFT per beat; regen rate; abandon-during-generation rate.
**Mitigation:** stream the regenerate too; gate only hard violations (leak/contradiction), never style; pre-render a diegetic beat ("she says nothing for a long moment") during regen; per-beat latency budget as a harness metric, as the brief already requires.
**KILL CRITERION — "THE DEAD-AIR CAP":** P95 time-to-first-visible-token > 5s, or regen rate > 15% sustained across a week → cut gate scope or change models before any growth spend.

### KR-11. Model-provider dependency, price and policy (P .30 × S 3 = 0.90)
**Narrative:** Two sub-risks. (a) *Price/access:* per-token prices have trended down, but the product's quality ceiling is one vendor's creative-writing quality; a deprecation or safety-tuning change can lobotomize the dramatizer overnight (AI Dungeon's history is the cautionary tale; their answer was owning open-weight finetunes — [Latitude/Forge](https://latitude.io/news/forge-a-new-era-of-ai-models-in-ai-dungeon)). (b) *Policy:* dramatized betrayal/violence/death sits near provider content-policy lines; the category's demand pressure (romance→NSFW) sits over them. Refuse it and lose whales; route it to open models and inherit the T&S blast radius.
**Early warning:** refusal/regen rate by provider version (receipts make this measurable — good); provider ToS diffs.
**Mitigation:** provider-agnostic gateway already designed; keep a validated open-weight fallback for the dramatizer from Phase 2; write the content-boundary decision (no NSFW, ever, at alpha) as policy now, not under pressure.
**KILL CRITERION — "THE REFUSAL CEILING":** if provider-caused refusals/quality regressions exceed 5% of beats for two consecutive weeks with no fix path, promote the open-weight fallback to primary regardless of quality delta.

### KR-12. Dataset legally encumbered by provider ToS (P .35 × S 4 = 1.40) — *not on the required list; add it*
**Narrative:** The sellable dataset as specified (choices + confirmed state + *the model-generated beats around them*) contains frontier-provider **outputs**. Major providers' terms restrict using outputs to train competing models — which is exactly what a purchasing lab would do. A lab's diligence will ask whose model text is in the corpus; if the answer is "a competitor's, generated under terms prohibiting this use," the asset's price collapses. This is a schema decision that must be made **now**: the resale-grade layer must be the human/state layer (choices, confirmations, edits, state deltas, rejected-option structure) with provider text either excluded, replaced by hashes/pointers, or generated by open-weight models whose licenses permit downstream training.
**Early warning:** none later — this is a now-or-never design item.
**Mitigation:** dual-layer dataset schema from day one; receipts already record provider+model per call (the plan's receipts save it here — extend them to record the ToS version in force).
**KILL CRITERION — "THE CLEAN-CORPUS GATE":** no dataset conversation with any buyer until an outside counsel memo confirms the export layer is unencumbered; if the human/state layer alone is judged insufficiently valuable, see KR-9's Comparable Test.

### KR-13. Benchmark ignored (P .60 × S 2 = 1.20)
**Narrative:** Vendor-published benchmarks where the vendor wins are marketing, and 2026 is saturated with them. "Gets cited in every competitor's diligence" (master plan §2) is hope, not mechanism.
**Early warning:** downloads, citations, third-party runs within two quarters of publication.
**Mitigation:** open-source the harness and scoring core; recruit one academic co-author; report conditions where the engine *loses* (there will be some — at short lengths, per KR-2, and that honesty is the credibility); score incumbent products, not just raw models, where ToS permits.
**KILL CRITERION — "THE CITATION CLOCK":** if two quarters post-publication there are no third-party runs or citations, stop investing; the harness stays as internal QA (where its real value was all along).

### KR-14. Regulatory reclassification as a companion chatbot (P .25 × S 4 = 1.00)
See §3.1. **KILL CRITERION — "THE COMPLIANCE GATE":** no launch in a state/jurisdiction without its checklist passed; if compliance cost for a jurisdiction exceeds its expected alpha value, geo-restrict rather than absorb.

**Ranked order (P×S):** KR-1 (2.25), KR-2 (2.00), KR-3 (2.00), KR-6 (1.80), KR-9 (1.65), KR-4 (1.40), KR-12 (1.40), KR-7 (1.35), KR-5 (1.25), KR-13 (1.20), KR-14 (1.00), KR-8 (0.90), KR-11 (0.90), KR-10 (0.75). Note the shape: the top five are *market and founder* risks, not engineering risks. The corpus spent 670k characters hardening the layer that was least likely to kill the company.

licensed worlds (Pride & Prejudice, Wizard of Oz, Call of Cthulhu, The Crow) on a rev-share licensing model ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/charliefink/2025/08/14/hidden-door-turns-fan-worlds-into-licensed-revenue-sharing-story-platforms/)); $9M raised, positive reception, "not yet scaled," and candid about "narrative ungroundedness" ([Mason & Todd](https://medium.com/@hmason/the-state-of-ai-native-games-lessons-from-the-frontier-3e696a9e3279)). *AI Dungeon/Latitude:* alive, iterating open-weight storytelling finetunes (Wayfarer Large on Llama 3.3 70B; Forge model era) ([Latitude](https://latitude.io/news/forge-a-new-era-of-ai-models-in-ai-dungeon); [AI Dungeon/X](https://x.com/aidungeon/status/1892296610589397469)). Category revenue context: AI companion apps ~$20–25M/month, conservatively (Mason & Todd).

### The three most dangerous, and their most likely counter-moves

**1) Character.AI — the feature-frame counter.** Most likely response to a StoryMachine press moment: extend Stories into "**Chapters**" for adults — endings, a "what your characters remember" panel, and "export your story" via transcript summarization — announced within a quarter, to 28M users, with the narrative "we already do this." They cannot cheaply rebuild memory as confirmable state at their scale, but users can't see architecture. **Pre-positioning that blunts it:** (a) publish the coherence *vocabulary and metrics* before they ship, so their version gets graded on your ruler (contradiction rate, payoff completion — the press will run the comparison for you); (b) own the audience they structurally can't serve: their brand is now legally and reputationally welded to teen-safety remediation — position StoryMachine as the *adult* (18+) drama product from day one; (c) exploit the incentive asymmetry: C.AI's business is session-minutes; real endings shorten sessions. They will always implement endings as a mode, not as physics. Your endings *are* the product. Say that sentence in every pitch.

**2) Sekai — the velocity counter.** Most likely response: a templated "interactive story with real endings" mini-app genre; 200k apps/day means a thousand shallow StoryMachine-alikes exist within weeks of the pattern being legible, and their remix culture strip-mines novel mechanics. **Pre-positioning:** don't compete on breadth — one world, absurdly polished, with an artifact whose *craft quality* (a compiled episode that reads like a produced script) is unreachable by prompt-assembled mini-apps; and persistence — Sekai's format is disposable-by-design, so multi-session serial arcs with kept state are the un-remixable feature. Also move fast on the name/category ("consequence physics," "stories that end"): in a remix economy, the originator label is worth real distribution.

**3) Talkie/MiniMax — the subsidy counter.** Most likely response: bolt a "story mode with endings" onto the companion graph, subsidized by in-house models at near-zero marginal cost, shipped to a US audience already at top-tier scale ([KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/talkies-global-traction-puts-minimax-in-ais-top-tier-but-risks-loom)). You cannot win a COGS war against a vertically integrated model owner. **Pre-positioning:** compliance and provenance as product: a Chinese-owned data pipeline is a standing diligence liability for every US acquirer and for US enterprise/lab data buyers (the TikTok-era climate did not relax); StoryMachine's consent-clean, US-jurisdiction, receipt-audited dataset is the thing MiniMax structurally cannot sell into a US lab. Build for the buyer's diligence, not for feature parity.

*(Hidden Door is the closest philosophical competitor — structured narrative, real arcs — but at $9M and sub-scale it is more plausible as a fellow-traveler, a validating data point, or an incumbent's acqui-target than a market threat. Watch for an incumbent acquiring them: that would be the fast-follow with actual architecture. AI Dungeon's Wayfarer line matters mainly as proof that open-weight dramatizers are viable — your KR-4/KR-11 escape hatch.)*

## 3. Legal / Regulatory Risk Map

### 3.1 Companion-chatbot law is here, and StoryMachine is plausibly in scope
Eight-plus US states have enacted chatbot/companion statutes ([Orrick, Apr 2026](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/04/2026-State-Chatbot-Laws-Key-Provisions-and-Regulatory-Trends)): **NY** (effective Nov 5, 2025 — AI-disclosure cadence, suicidal-ideation detection, crisis referral), **CA SB 243** (Jan 1, 2026 — disclosure, minor protections, crisis protocols; Office of Suicide Prevention reporting from Jul 1, 2027; **private right of action, $1,000/violation minimum** — [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/10/new-california-companion-chatbot-law)), **TN** (Jul 2026), **OR** (Jan 2027, PRA), **WA** (Jan 2027, **PRA** — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/washington-state-enacts-law-regulating-ai-companion-chatbots-with-private-right-of-action)), **NE/ID** (Jul 2027). Federal: the **GUARD Act** (S.3062 — minors ban + age verification for AI companions) is pending ([Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/3062/text)); the FTC has a running 6(b) study of AI companions ([CLA](https://calawyers.org/privacy-law/regulatory-focus-on-ai-companion-character-chatbots/)).
**The classification question:** SB 243 defines a companion chatbot as one giving "adaptive, human-like responses" capable of "sustain[ing] a relationship across multiple interactions," and exempts **video-game bots only where conversation is limited to game-specific topics** ([Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/10/new-california-companion-chatbot-law)). StoryMachine has a genuine game-exemption argument — bounded stories, endings, characters that exist only inside a narrative frame — and the "stories END" design is a real regulatory asset (no infinite parasocial loop; the GUARD-era harm model is *endless* companionship). But characters players talk to across sessions, in free text, will *sustain relationships*; a court or AG may not care about your ontology. **Posture: comply as if covered** (disclosure, crisis detection/referral, minor exclusion, logs), and keep the game-exemption argument as a defense, not a plan. **EU:** AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations became applicable **Aug 2, 2026** — AI-interaction disclosure and machine-readable marking of AI-generated content ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-08-03-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-take-effect-2-august-2026); [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-starts-enforcing-ai-act-rules-and-new-transparency-requirements-2-august)). If serving the EU at alpha, disclosure + artifact marking are live duties this week; a US-only alpha is defensible and simpler.

### 3.2 Minors and age verification
Recommendation: **18+ only at alpha**, with reasonable age assurance, for four stacked reasons: (1) every state statute's sharpest teeth are minor-specific; (2) the GUARD Act would mandate verification for companions; (3) app-store age-verification laws are arriving — Texas's is **in effect** after SCOTUS declined to block it, with UT/LA/AK-class laws stalled into 2027 ([DMNews](https://dmnews.com/n-texass-app-store-age-verification-law-remains-in-effect-after-the-supreme-court-declined-to-block-it-making-it-the-first-of-four-state-app-store-age-verification-laws-to-actually-take-effect-while/); [FPF comparison chart](https://fpf.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/FPF-Legislation-TX-UT-LA-App-Store-Accountability-Act-Comparison-Chart.pdf)) — mobile-web-first conveniently sidesteps app-store obligations for now, but a native app later inherits them; (4) **the dataset**: any plausible minor data in the corpus poisons its resale value permanently (no lab will touch it, no diligence will clear it). 18+ is not a T&S posture; it is asset protection.

### 3.3 Dataset consent — the exit asset's non-retrofittable clauses
The 23andMe bankruptcy set the precedent: user data *can* be sold as an asset, but the FTC and 27+ state AGs made clear the **buyer inherits the seller's privacy promises**, and the consent language at collection time governs everything ([NatLawReview/FTC letter](https://natlawreview.com/article/privacy-tip-438-ftc-chairman-shares-concerns-over-23andme-data); [Regulatory Oversight](https://www.regulatoryoversight.com/2025/04/state-ags-and-the-ftc-warn-of-23andme-risks-following-bankruptcy-announcement/); [Loeb, ombudsman report](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/23andme-bankruptcy-the-privacy-ombudsmans-report)). **Day-one requirements for a sellable dataset:**
1. ToS/Privacy Policy stating explicitly that gameplay interactions, choices, and edits are used **to train and improve AI models** and may be **licensed or transferred to third parties, including AI developers,** in de-identified/aggregated form — named plainly, not buried.
2. An **assignment/successor clause**: data transfers in merger, acquisition, or asset sale bind the successor to the same commitments. Corollary from 23andMe: **never over-promise** ("we will never share your data" is a poison pill you drink at exit).
3. A **separate, unbundled opt-in/opt-out** for dataset contribution (GDPR-grade consent if EU users are ever served; otherwise geo-fence EU data out of the export layer). Retroactively broadening ToS to cover training is an FTC unfair-practice landmine — get it right at signup one.
4. **CCPA**: disclose sale/sharing, honor opt-outs and GPC signals; treat roleplay text as potentially **sensitive PI** (free text will contain orientation, health, religion, confessions) with a PII-scrubbing pipeline before any export.
5. **Deletion propagation**: per-user tombstones that excise deleted users from all future dataset exports, contractually flowed down to any buyer.
6. **Provenance layering (KR-12):** the export layer contains human choices/edits/state only; provider-generated text is excluded or replaced by content hashes; receipts record model + ToS version per generation. This is the difference between an asset and an exhibit.
7. **18+ certification** records for the entire contribution pool (§3.2).

### 3.4 IP posture
- **Inbound (UGC worlds):** "original and user-created worlds only" is policy, not protection — users will build Hogwarts with the serial numbers filed off. Need: reps/warranties in creator terms, a trademark/famous-IP filter at world creation, DMCA agent registration (trivial cost, mandatory for safe harbor, do it now), takedown workflow. Hidden Door's licensed-rev-share model ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/charliefink/2025/08/14/hidden-door-turns-fan-worlds-into-licensed-revenue-sharing-story-platforms/)) means "clean differentiation" is honest — but phrase it as *different strategy*, not moral high ground; theirs is consensual licensing, not infringement.
- **Outbound (artifacts):** compiled episodes will be posted, printed, maybe sold by users. Artifact license terms needed now: user owns their compiled story text; platform retains a license to display share pages; watermark/provenance marking (which the EU AI Act now wants anyway).
- **Exemplar corpus:** the licensing-clean policy (PD/owned/commissioned) is right; the diligence gap is *proof* — keep signed commissioned-work agreements and PD provenance notes per exemplar, from the first one.

### 3.5 WGA / Hollywood optics (second-act pro tool)
Verified 2026 state: the WGA entered 2026 negotiations seeking **payment for AI training on writers' scripts** ([Variety, 2026](https://variety.com/2026/film/news/wga-ai-training-amptp-talks-1236684012/)) and reached a **tentative four-year deal** in early April — strike averted ([NPR, Apr 5 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/04/05/nx-s1-5774517); [THR](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/writers-guild-tentative-four-year-deal-studios-1236555949/)). Implications: (a) the temperature is lower than a strike year but AI-training compensation is now a *bargained-for right* — a pro tool whose dataset ingests writers' drafts without explicit paid consent is radioactive; (b) therefore the **pro surface's drafts must never enter the training/export dataset** absent a separate written opt-in — build the exclusion flag into the schema now; (c) position the second act as writer-controlled tooling (the confirmation architecture is genuinely aligned with WGA's "AI can't be the author" framing — the human confirms all truth; that's a marketing asset with the guild, use it); (d) never demo the consumer product as "generates screenplays without writers."

## 5. The Three Hardest Questions

**Q1. "Character.AI has 28M MAU and already shipped Stories. Sekai ships 200,000 apps a day. When this works, they copy it in a quarter — why do you exist in 18 months?"**
*Strongest honest answer:* "They'll copy the nouns — endings, memory, script export. Two things don't copy. First, incentives: their businesses monetize session-minutes, and real endings shorten sessions — C.AI shipped Stories as a *safety remediation for minors*, not as their core loop, and every incumbent implementation of 'endings' will be a mode bolted onto an engagement machine. Our endings are the physics, so completion and artifact quality are our native metrics and their vanity metrics. Second, the records: a consent-clean, state-conditioned choice dataset and a published, open-scored coherence benchmark are accumulating assets — a fast-follower starts both at zero on the day they copy, and their copy gets measured on our published ruler. And candidly: if a 28M-MAU platform decides this category matters that much, the fastest way for them to get it is the transaction this company is built for."

**Q2. "Name a comparable transaction for your dataset. Who has ever bought state-conditioned narrative preference data, and at what price?"**
*Strongest honest answer:* "No direct comparable exists — I won't pretend one does. The adjacent markets are real: labs spend heavily on human preference data through vendors, and license platform corpora (Reddit-scale deals) — but vendor data is commissioned from paid annotators with no real stakes, and platform corpora have no state annotation. Ours is organic, longitudinal, real-stakes choice data with ground-truth story state at decision time and unambiguous consent — the version of this data that can't be commissioned. I price it honestly: it is a multiplier on the acquisition and a COGS-reducer internally — we use it to fine-tune our own cheap dramatizer, which is a value floor independent of any lab's interest. It is not a standalone pillar, and our deck doesn't lean on it as one." *(This answer only survives if KR-12's clean-corpus schema shipped on day one.)*

**Q3. "A teenager died in this category, eight states have statutes, two with private rights of action — and you're one person. Why should anyone believe you can operate this safely, and what happens to the company if you get hit by a bus?"**
*Strongest honest answer:* "Because the architecture is the safety system, and it leaves records. Irreversible acts interrupt and ask; every generation carries a receipt; crisis detection and 18+ age assurance shipped before the first public user; the incident log, crisis-trigger log, and moderation SLAs have existed since alpha — I can show them, not describe them. The alpha is capped to what one operator plus automation can genuinely moderate, and the first post-funding hire is T&S ops, not engineering. On key-person risk: the kernel is deterministic, golden-tested, and documented to be operable without me — which is also exactly what makes this acquirable rather than an acqui-hire of one. I won't dodge the premise: solo operation in this category is a temporary state with a countdown attached, and the plan treats it that way." *(This answer is only as good as KR-3's discipline: if the logs don't exist, nothing said in that room matters.)*
