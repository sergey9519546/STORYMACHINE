# Dimension Honesty Audit — 2026-07-28

**Status:** Evidence-only. No engine, formula, rule, or detector was
modified. One new measurement script (`scripts/probe-dimension-honesty.mjs`)
and its output (`scripts/output/dimension-honesty.csv`) are added.

## Purpose

`READINESS_ASSESSMENT_2026-07-28.md` established that the health score
discriminates on 2 of 5 channels (dialogue, scene boundaries) and is blind
on 3 (global arc, 3-act structure, climax ordering) at the *whole-score*
level. That left an unanswered question: **the report a writer sees is
per-dimension and per-claim, not a single score.** Does each dimension
*label* and each "What's Working" *claim* match what the underlying signal
actually measures?

This audit answers that by:

1. Running the five degradations against the **sample report's own script**
   (what P0 writers actually see) and printing **per-dimension** deltas,
   not just whole-score deltas.
2. Tracing each "What's Working" claim to its generator function and asking:
   is the claim a *verdict* (must be supportable by the score) or a
   *description of a gated signal* (honest if the gate is real)?
3. Falsifying each position-dependent claim by applying the matching
   degradation and checking whether the claim *disappears*.

## Probe 3 — Per-dimension deltas

**Script:** `scripts/probe-dimension-honesty.mjs`
**Output:** `scripts/output/dimension-honesty.csv`
**Command:** `node scripts/probe-dimension-honesty.mjs`

Baseline dimensions on the sample script (`src/lib/sample-script.ts`,
"The Second Key", 14 scenes):

| Dimension | Baseline score |
|---|---:|
| Structure & Pacing | 67.7 |
| Character | 67.7 |
| Dialogue & Voice | 95.5 |
| Plot Logic & Payoff | 69.9 |
| Theme & Originality | 98.8 |

Per-dimension delta under each degradation (sample script):

| Degradation | Structure & Pacing | Character | Dialogue & Voice | Plot Logic & Payoff | Theme & Originality |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| SCENE_SHUFFLE | +0.6 | +0.6 | −0.3 | **−8.9** | 0 |
| MIDPOINT_DROP | **−8.1** | +0.3 | −1.8 | −3.1 | −0.3 |
| CLIMAX_RELOCATE | −2.2 | −5.5 | −1.5 | −1.1 | 0 |
| DIALOGUE_FLATTEN | −16.3 | **−25.5** | −5.2 | −4.2 | −1.5 |
| SCENE_MERGE | +31.3 | +32.3 | +1.5 | +30.1 | +1.2 |

### Reading the matrix

- **Dialogue channel is strongly live in the Character and Structure
  dimensions**, not just "Dialogue & Voice." Flattening dialogue drops
  Character by 25.5 points — the protagonist-agency and voice signals
  genuinely read dialogue. This is honest and strong.
- **Plot Logic & Payoff responds to scene shuffle (−8.9)** — the causality
  and payoff passes do read cross-scene ordering at this script length.
  This is a real signal at 14 scenes.
- **Structure & Pacing responds to midpoint drop (−8.1)** at this length —
  but note the feature-scale result below.
- **Theme & Originality barely moves under anything (max −1.5).** It is
  effectively a ceiling dimension on this script; its 98.8 score is not
  sensitive to any of these damages. Whether that is "correct" (the sample
  is genuinely thematically rich) or "blind" (the dimension can't see
  thematic damage) is not decidable from this probe alone.
- **SCENE_MERGE raises every dimension** (+31 across the board on
  Structure/Character/Plot). This is the `140/sceneCount` scarcity term
  running in reverse: merging scenes collapses scene count, which
  *reduces* the scarcity penalty, which *raises* the score. This is a
  known formula property (NORTH_STAR §2 law #2), not a dimension bug —
  but it means a writer who deletes all their scene headings would see
  their score *improve*, which is the opposite of honest.

### Feature-scale vs sample-scale (important caveat)

The sample script is 14 scenes. The same degradations on **Ratatouille**
(127 scenes) show near-zero movement on every structural channel:

| Degradation | Ratatouille Structure & Pacing Δ | Sample Structure & Pacing Δ |
|---|---:|---:|
| SCENE_SHUFFLE | 0.0 | +0.6 |
| MIDPOINT_DROP | −0.4 | −8.1 |
| CLIMAX_RELOCATE | 0.0 | −2.2 |

This is the density-normalization absorption NORTH_STAR §2 law #2
describes: at feature scale, the opportunity-normalized formula treats
issue count relative to script length, and a 25-scene structural gap
collapses to <1 point of health. **The structural dimensions are
partially sensitive at short-script scale and effectively blind at
feature scale.** The sample report happens to sit in the sensitive regime,
which is fortunate for P0 but means the report's structural sensitivity
will not generalize to a writer's real feature draft.

## Probe 4 — Claim falsification

Each "What's Working" bullet in the sample report
(`docs/user-validation/sample-coverage-report.html` lines 371-376) is
generated by a deterministic function in `server/nvm/analyze/doctor.ts`.
The honesty question is narrower than "does the score move": **does each
claim *disappear* when the property it asserts is destroyed?**

| # | Claim (abbreviated) | Generator | Gate | Disappears under damage? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Tension rises first→second half" | `buildTensionRiseStrength` | real (compares half-averages) | not tested — content-preserving shuffle can preserve this |
| 2 | "Every planted clue paid off" | `buildPayoffCompletenessStrength` | real (tracks clue IDs) | likely robust — payoff tracking is content-level |
| 3 | "Clock resurfaces in both halves" | `buildClockPersistenceStrength` | real (scene-position check) | **partially position-dependent** |
| 4 | "6 distinct scene functions" | `buildFunctionDistributionStrength` | real (distribution check) | robust — content-level |
| 5 | "Suspense builds to peak late" | `buildSuspenseShapingStrength` | real (quartile averages) | **position-dependent** |
| 6 | "Climax is where it belongs" | `buildClimaxPlacementStrength` | real (peak in final 30%) | **NO — see below** |

### The climax claim: a real, gated signal that survives its own falsification

Claim #6 is the strongest assertion in the report. Its generator
(`doctor.ts:1303-1317`) computes per-scene intensity
(`suspenseDelta + clockDelta + dramaticTurn`) and only emits the claim if
the peak intensity scene is in the final 30% AND the last quarter
outweighs the first. This is a genuine position-reading gate — not an
unconditional emission.

I tested it by relocating the final scene (the baseline peak, intensity 3
at scene index 12) to position 2. Result:

```
Baseline intensity:   [0,0,0,1,0,2,2,1,0,1,1,1,3,0]  peak=3 at idx 12
Relocated intensity:  [0,0,0,0,1,0,2,2,1,0,1,1,1,3]  peak=3 at idx 13
Claim after relocate: STILL PRESENT
```

The claim survives because **relocating the climax scene drops its own
intensity to 0** (its `suspenseDelta` was partly position-derived) while
**the new last scene (formerly index 12) inherits intensity 3** and
becomes the new peak — still in the final 30%. So `buildClimaxPlacement
Strength` fires again, correctly by its own logic.

**The honesty gap is in the claim's wording, not its gate.** A writer
reads "the climax is where it belongs" and understands "my specific
climax scene is correctly placed." The engine means "whichever scene is
most intense happens to sit late." These are different propositions. The
gate is real; the claim *overstates* what the gate proves. Under a
degradation that destroys climax placement, the claim does not retract —
it silently re-binds to whatever scene is now most intense.

This is the same class of issue NORTH_STAR §2 law #1 names for lexicon
signals: the signal carries *content* (which scene has the most
suspense-language) but only weakly carries *position* (whether the
author's intended climax is the one that registered). The bounded
structural-deduction pathway (P1) is the documented fix for exactly this.

## Fielding recommendation (refined)

The score is not dishonest in the sense the retracted draft feared — it
does not report a dialogue dimension computed from zero dialogue, and the
"What's Working" claims are gated on real signals, not emitted
unconditionally. The honesty issues are subtler and fall into two
categories:

### Category A — Wording overstatement (fix before fielding)

Claims whose **gate is real but whose wording asserts more than the gate
proves.** These are honest-degradation violations in spirit if not in
letter: a writer would reasonably infer a stronger guarantee than the
engine provides.

- **Claim #6** ("The climax is where it belongs") — gate checks "some
  intense scene is late," wording implies "the author's climax is
  placed correctly." Fix: soften to "The draft's most intense scene
  lands in the final stretch" (drop "where it belongs" / "climax").
- **Claim #3** ("The clock resurfaces in both halves") — position-
  dependent; verify the gate survives scene reorder, soften if not.

### Category B — Scale-dependent sensitivity (document, don't fix)

Dimensions that **respond to damage at short-script scale but not feature
scale** due to density normalization. This is a documented formula
property (NORTH_STAR §2 law #2), not a bug. The P0 sample script sits in
the sensitive regime, so the sample report is honest *for the sample*.
The risk is generalization: a writer's real feature draft will not show
the same structural sensitivity.

Fix: add a one-line caveat to the report footer or the dimension
summaries when sceneCount exceeds the absorption threshold, e.g.
"Structural signals are most reliable on scripts under ~40 scenes; at
feature length, focus on the dialogue and per-scene findings." This is a
documentation change, not an engine change.

### Net recommendation

**P0 can be fielded after the Category A wording fixes.** The fixes are
to claim-string templates in `doctor.ts` (e.g., line 1316), which are
presentation-layer — but per the P0 freeze, even presentation-layer
engine edits are blocked without explicit approval. The recommendation is
therefore: **approve a narrow, named exception to the freeze for the
Category A claim-wording fixes only**, then field. Category B is a
documentation caveat that can ship alongside or after.
